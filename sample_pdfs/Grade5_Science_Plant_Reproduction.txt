CHAPTER 4: PLANT REPRODUCTION AND GERMINATION
Subject: Grade 5 General Science

1. Reproduction in Bryophyllum:
Bryophyllum reproduces vegetatively through its leaves. Small plant buds grow along the margins of the leaf. When these buds fall onto moist soil, each bud develops into a new independent plant.

2. Process of Pollination:
Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the male anther to the female stigma of a flower. Agents of pollination include wind, water, insects, and birds.

3. Role of Cotyledons:
Cotyledons are the seed leaves stored inside a seed. They store nutrients and supply food to the baby plant (embryo) during germination until the plant grows green leaves to make its own food.

4. Seed Dispersal by Water:
Water helps float seeds and fruits from one place to another. Examples include Coconut and Lotus fruits, which have fibrous or spongy outer coats that help them float on rivers and ocean currents.

5. Vegetative Propagation:
Vegetative propagation is an asexual method of plant reproduction where new plants grow from vegetative parts of the parent plant such as roots, stems, and leaves instead of seeds.

6. Essential Requirements for Seed Germination:
Germination is the process by which a seed sprouts into a seedling. The three essential conditions required for seed germination are water, air (oxygen), and suitable warmth (temperature).

7. Seed Water Absorption:
When a seed is soaked in water, it absorbs water through its seed hole (micropyle) and swells, causing the seed coat to burst open so the root (radicle) can emerge.
